**Prepared:** 2026-10-29. **Status: DRAFT — owner approval required before scoring implementation.** These are proposed product assessment standards, not verified investment advice, jurisdictional law, expert-approved reference judgments or a claim of model quality. No production framework is activated by this document. The candidate makes the remaining policy decision reviewable rather than asking a developer/model to invent anchors later.

## 1. Proposed policy package

Retain the existing 24 criterion IDs, six domains and four verdicts. Adopt the existing proposed 60% evidence coverage, every applicable essential criterion assessable, and at least `ceil(applicableCriteria/2)` assessable criteria for a domain score. Criterion scores are integers 1–5; eligible domain mean uses equal weights and decimal half-up rounding to one decimal. Compare thresholds before rounding. No overall investment score, scenario average, evidence-quality upgrade from manual input or duplicate corroboration.

All six domains remain visible. Missing evidence is Not Assessable, never score 1. Unsupported stage or legal context withholds affected scoring only. NA needs a recorded factual applicability reason; empty denominators have no percentage. An approved provisional opinion remains provisional. Technically incomplete results cannot be approved and export only as Draft/Incomplete with every-page labels. A human can approve their own assessment; two-person approval is not mandatory.

The compact form subset and Persian terminology already in product §§6.1 and 17.4 are the initial candidate. Persian and English inputs, Persian UI calendar/Tehran timestamps, and preserved source calendars/units remain separate contracts. Scenario representation is up to three alternatives per conflict and five material groups; exceeding the coherent representation withholds affected outcomes rather than hiding conflicts or generating a Cartesian product.

## 2. Evidence/eligibility policy

Each criterion has four separately recorded evidence requirements, with stable IDs `{criterion}.R1` through `.R4`:

1. **R1 scope:** dated, attributed statement of the criterion's subject, business context and scope. A manual assertion can satisfy attribution/scope but not independently prove operating performance.
2. **R2 direct record:** the criterion-specific records below, appropriate to the stage basis in §3. Document text must actually support the assessed proposition; a pitch-deck assertion alone cannot replace an operational, financial, ownership or test record.
3. **R3 interpretation context:** definitions, period/as-of date and relevant denominator, methodology or operating conditions. Missing context that would materially change the finding makes this requirement insufficient.
4. **R4 consistency review:** identified supporting/challenging records, duplicate-origin check and material conflicts explicitly represented. A recorded search of the selected set with “no independent corroboration supplied” may satisfy the review procedure, but does not establish factual corroboration or meet a stronger R2 source requirement.

R1–R3 are essential evidence for scoring a criterion; R4 is also required before publishing its score, because unreviewed contradictions must not disappear. Each requirement is Satisfied/Insufficient/Missing/justified NA. A supported adverse observation can satisfy evidence requirements; “satisfied evidence” does not mean good performance. Every assessed alternative uses the same quality rules. The domain 60% threshold therefore describes coverage across criteria, not fractional confidence invented by AI.

**Proposed freshness:** scope/context confirmed as of assessment; current operational/financial/team records dated within the latest 90 days where claiming current state; historical performance records cover the explicitly claimed period and may be older without becoming false. Legal/ownership/contract artifacts use effective dates and a dated current-state/change assertion, not an arbitrary expiry. A record outside the relevant freshness window remains historical evidence but cannot alone establish a current-state score. Unsupported dates remain gaps. The owner/domain reviewer may amend these product thresholds before approval.

**Proposed essential criteria:** C1/C3, P1/P2, T1/T3, F1/F2, O1/O2, L1/L2 across supported stages. Explicitly justified NA removes an essential criterion from the applicable set; unknown or missing evidence does not. For a non-revenue pre-commercial startup F1 may be NA only with attributed evidence of that operating state, not merely an empty revenue field. No blanket NA for unincorporated entities, security, ownership or legal context.

## 5. Opinion, confidence and risk rules

- Confidence is qualitative High/Medium/Low with explanation, not an investment-success probability. High requires direct current coherent evidence and meaningful independent corroboration for consequential assertions; Medium meets assessability with bounded limitations; Low exposes material uncertainty and cannot override failed eligibility.
- Use the existing four verdict meanings in P §12.2. No mechanical score-to-verdict mapping. Each directional verdict must cite accepted findings across the assessed scope, adverse evidence, material risks, missing domains and scenario implications. Empty/unusable evidence yields Insufficient Evidence. A defensible direction can be provisional even when some domains are unassessable; explain why.
- Critical risk follows P §11.2's viability/essential-rights/opinion-basis definition and requires acknowledgement; High/Medium/Low/Undetermined retain their existing meanings. A missing document is normally a gap, not proof of an adverse event. Acceptance never hides a risk; mitigation/resolution needs qualifying locked evidence.
- If coherent unresolved scenarios imply different verdicts and no common defensible direction can be supported, use Insufficient Evidence with scenario-specific implications. Do not choose the most favorable alternative. Supported scenario differences remain visible even when the same overall verdict is defensible.
- Saving substantive changes invalidates current opinion dependencies/approval. Changed material content requires new acknowledgement; opening/cancelling does not. New facts require a successor. Human score overrides still obey eligibility and require existing evidence/rationale.
